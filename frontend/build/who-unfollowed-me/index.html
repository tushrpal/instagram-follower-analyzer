Who Unfollowed Me on Instagram?

Why You Can't See Unfollowers in the Instagram App

Instagram does not provide a dated unfollow event in the export used by this tracker. A current followers list shows who is present at that snapshot, not who left earlier. Without an older snapshot, a missing account cannot be classified as a recent unfollower.

This is why so many third-party "who unfollowed me" apps exist. The vast majority of them are unsafe — they ask for your Instagram username and password to scrape your followers list on a schedule, which violates Instagram's Terms of Service and exposes your account to bans, hacks, and data leaks.

The Safe Way: Use Your Own Instagram Data Export

Instagram provides a download-your-information feature that can include follower and following lists. Each export is a point-in-time input. Its completeness and structure depend on what Instagram provides and the options you select.

The tracker compares two exports from different dates. An account in the older followers set but not the newer set is classified as a lost follower between those snapshots. Account deletion, renaming, or missing export data can also affect that difference, so treat it as a snapshot comparison rather than a record of intent.

Step-by-Step: How to See Who Unfollowed You

  1. Open Instagram → Settings → Your activity → Download your information.
  2. Click Request a download, then choose Some of your information.
  3. Under the Connections section, select only Followers and following.
  4. Set the date range to All time and the format to JSON.
  5. Submit the request and wait for Instagram to notify you when the export is ready. Preparation time varies.
  6. Download the ZIP file and upload it to our free tracker.
  7. Repeat the process at a later date. Compare the second export with the first to identify usernames that left the followers set between those snapshots.

Why This Method Beats Every Other "Who Unfollowed Me" Tool

Apps that scrape Instagram's API have three big problems. First, they only see a snapshot of your followers right now — they cannot reach backward in time to compare against past data, so when you install them they have nothing to compare against and you have to wait days or weeks for the first meaningful result. Second, they're rate-limited by Instagram and frequently miss accounts. Third, they require your password.

Export comparison avoids sharing an Instagram password with the tracker. It can only compare snapshots you actually possess; a newly requested export does not reconstruct an older followers list. Results depend on both exports and may be affected by account deletion, renaming, or format changes.

Does It Work for Private Instagram Accounts?

The tracker can analyse follower information included in an export from your own private account because it does not rely on public profile visibility. The exact contents remain controlled by Instagram's export and the options you request.
